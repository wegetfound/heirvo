# Memory Vault — Light Variant

## Why TV+ editorial language on light

Apple TV+'s grammar — hero card on top, curated horizontal rails below — is a story-first layout. It frames content as something *worth being moved by*, not a database to be queried. For a non-technical, often older audience preserving family legacy, that framing matters more than density. Inverting it to a warm cream palette (Fraunces serif on `#F8F4EC`) removes the cinematic-streaming-service coldness and replaces it with something closer to a printed memoir or a coffee-table photo book — daylight, paper, ink. The "cinematic" feeling is carried by layout scale, editorial type, and generous whitespace rather than by darkness.

## What's distinctive vs. the alternatives

- **vs. a Photos-style grid**: a grid says "here is everything, you find it." This says "here is what we found *for you* today." The hero card ("On this day in 1999 — Hawaii") is the entire emotional proposition — serendipity over exhaustiveness.
- **vs. a search-first interface**: search is still one keystroke away in the nav, but the home screen leads with rediscovery, because most users don't yet know what they're looking for. They want to be reminded.

## Interaction principles

- One emotional headline per screen — never two competing focal points.
- Transcripts are presented as *prose*, not subtitles, because they read as memory, not as metadata.
- Hover and motion are restrained (1px lifts, soft shadows). The content does the work.

## Tradeoffs

Density is sacrificed deliberately — a power user with 500 discs would want a list view, which isn't designed here. And horizontal scrolling rails are slower to scan than grids; acceptable because the goal is *encounter*, not *retrieval*.
