> The system for recruiting, screening, paying, and quality-controlling a nationwide
> network of home-based disc-recovery lab operators. This is the source document for

## 1. The model in one page

Heirvo runs a mail-in disc-recovery service: customers mail damaged DVDs, CDs, and Kodak
Photo CDs holding irreplaceable family memories; we recover the files on professional
optical equipment and deliver them via cloud link, USB, or upload to Google Photos / iCloud.
Promise: **no recovery, no charge.**

The Lab Network turns the existing "we'll send you your nearest regional address" promise
into a real operational backbone. Each **lab operator** works from home with a laptop and
two specified DVD drives (a Pioneer + a LiteOn) running Heirvo's software, and does the
actual extraction work. Heirvo provides the brand, the software, customer acquisition,
payment processing, order routing, and centralized QC. The operator provides labor, their
own equipment, a secure workspace, and careful handling.

**The core design principle:** the standardized hardware + software flattens the *technical*
skill curve, so the entire screening and quality system is built around the one failure mode
that actually matters — **catastrophic loss or destruction of an irreplaceable original.**
That single event (a destroyed only-copy wedding video) is the brand-killer. Everything below
exists to prevent it, not to test raw competence.

**Four interlocking pieces, each reinforcing the others:**

- **Territory** is *granted, not sold* — earned by passing a paid trial, held as a revocable
  performance lease. It is the retention carrot (loss aversion), the quality enforcer (lose it
  if you slip), and the liability backstop (your license is the collateral, not your savings).
- **Pay** is tiered piece-rate by difficulty + an attempt fee on logged failures + a monthly
  quality bonus — structured so the careful, thorough path is always the most profitable one.
- **Liability** follows the protocol: follow the logged chain-of-custody and Heirvo covers any
  loss; violate it and it becomes a strike against your standing.
- **The equipment the operator buys (~$150–250) is the skin-in-the-game** — the commitment
  filter that replaces a franchise fee, and (critically) keeps Heirvo out of franchise
  classification.

## 2. Positioning — why this wins

The memory/disc market splits into three tiers, and Heirvo sits in a gap none of them fill:

- **Centralized mail-in warehouses (true competitors):** **Legacybox** (category leader,
  ~$66 for 2 items up to ~$1,180 for 40) is beatable — 1.6–2.5★ across review sites, an
  *Inside Edition* investigation over **lost media**, and turnaround that slips from a stated
  4–6 weeks to "three to five months" in complaints. **iMemories** is faster/cheaper
  ($19.99/disc) but draws "fuzzy output" and bad-support complaints. Both put your originals
  in an anonymous pile a thousand miles away.
- **Forensic data recovery:** DriveSavers / Secure Data Recovery do recover optical media but
  as part of $700–$3,000+ enterprise jobs — wildly overpriced and emotionally cold for a
  scratched family DVD.
- **Commodity photo channels:** ScanMyPhotos, Walgreens, (Costco exited in 2023) — these
  *digitize working media*; none recover damaged/unreadable discs.

**The white space nobody owns:** "We recover the disc you thought was *dead* — the scratched,
rotted, unreadable Kodak Photo CD — fast, with a named human who handles *your* disc, and your
originals never vanish into a warehouse pile."

**Lead the message on the three things every incumbent is weak on:** recovery of
*damaged/unreadable* media (a capability, not a commodity), **speed** ("days, not months"
against Legacybox's backlog), and **named-human accountability**.

**The distributed model's own risks** — and the mitigations baked into this system:
consistency (→ standardized hardware + centralized QC before files ship), trust-at-the-door
(→ tracking, photo-on-receipt, insurance, the central guarantee behind every operator), and
chain-of-custody in a home (→ the mandatory logged protocol in §8).

## 3. The operator lifecycle

```
Apply  →  Integrity/Conscientiousness screen  →  PAID trial disc  →  Background check
      →  Acceptance + territory granted  →  Probation (100% QC)  →  Graduated autonomy
      →  Ongoing (sampling QC + blind re-test discs + monthly bonus)  →  [strikes → revocation]
```

Each stage is cheaper than letting a bad actor through the next one. No operator ever ships
files to a customer unreviewed during probation.

## 4. Screening system

**What actually predicts success:** not intelligence — **conscientiousness + integrity.** The
meta-analytic record is strong (integrity tests predict job performance at r ≈ .41 and predict
*counterproductive behavior* — theft, dishonesty, rule-breaking — better than almost any other
single screen; conscientiousness at r ≈ .31). For a job whose failure mode is "cut a corner and
lose an heirloom," these are the right levers, not a coding test.

**Five-stage funnel:**

1. **Application + self-selection.** Temperament questions, not résumé: *"Describe a time you
   were responsible for something irreplaceable that belonged to someone else."* / *"Walk
   through how you'd handle realizing you damaged a customer's only disc."* / *"Describe your
   dedicated, lockable workspace"* (require a photo). Reject anyone who treats the items as
   commodities.
2. **Integrity/conscientiousness screen.** A personality-based integrity instrument (loads on
   conscientiousness, agreeableness, emotional stability) — harder to game than an overt "do you
   steal?" test. This is a **gate**, not a tiebreaker.
3. **The PAID trial disc** — the single most predictive stage. Mail a **known-condition disc**
   we've already fully recovered and graded internally, seeded with realistic defects (a
   scratch, a partially unreadable session). **Pay them** for the work (this is the paid trial
   that earns territory — never charge for it; see §11 legal). Grade four dimensions:
   - **Output fidelity** — does their extraction match our master file-for-file? (Pass/fail.)
   - **Handling** — require photo/video of unboxing, disc surface, and re-packing. Gloves?
     Photographed condition on arrival? Stored in a sleeve?
   - **Chain-of-custody discipline** — each step logged, timestamped, labeled.
   - **Communication + honesty** — *the integrity tell:* seed a disc whose correct answer is
     *partial* recovery, and see whether they honestly report what they couldn't get or quietly
     hide it. **Hiding a failure = instant reject regardless of output quality.**
4. **Background check.** SSN trace, county + federal + nationwide criminal, sex-offender search
   (mirrors Snapdocs' "Verified" standard). Items enter homes and leave with personal data —
   non-negotiable, and a marketing asset ("every operator is background-verified").
5. **Probation with 100% QC, then graduated autonomy.** The first ~25 real jobs are 100%
   re-verified by HQ before files ship. Autonomy is earned in tiers:
   **100% review → 50% → 20% sampling → trusted-tier** (with a permanent random floor; see §8).

## 5. Acceptance bar

Explicit, published criteria to be granted a territory:

- Passed the integrity/conscientiousness screen above threshold.
- **Trial disc: 100% output fidelity** (a single lost/corrupted file = fail — this job has no
  "B+"), **AND** handling/custody score ≥ 95%, **AND** honest reporting of the seeded defect.
- Clean background check.
- A **dedicated, lockable, single-purpose workspace** (photo-verified) — no shared kitchen
  tables, no roommates with access.
- Owns both required drives; passes a hardware/software calibration check.
- Signs the operator agreement: chain-of-custody protocol, "originals are sacred" handling
  rules, data-confidentiality/NDA, and acknowledgment that **territory is a revocable
  performance lease, not property.**

## 6. Removal criteria — "what gets you booted"

**Instant termination (territory revoked immediately):**

- Dishonesty of any kind — concealing damage/loss, faking custody logs, falsifying results.
- Loss or destruction of an original through protocol breach.
- Working outside the secured workspace; letting an unauthorized person handle items.
- Exfiltrating or retaining customer data; any privacy breach.
- A background-disqualifying event during tenure.

**Escalating strikes (quality/SLA, not integrity):**

- **Strike 1 — Coaching:** missed SLA, sloppy log, minor handling lapse caught in QC.
  Documented warning + retraining.
- **Strike 2 — Probation:** sampling snaps back to 100% review (at the operator's own time
  cost); written improvement plan.
- **Strike 3 — Territory revocation.** Three quality strikes in a rolling window, or a quality
  score below the network floor.

Tie strikes to a visible **Operator Score** built from a *predictive composite* — custody
compliance %, re-verify pass rate, SLA hit rate, customer feedback — **not raw stars** (rating
inflation is real: Field Nation found 81% of providers at perfect 5.0).

## 7. Pay structure

Operator cut targets **~30–46% of recovery revenue** — generous enough to retain reliable home
labs, lean enough to protect Heirvo's gross. Difficulty tier is assigned by **Heirvo's software
log** (passes attempted, error-correction depth, surface-clean flag), **not operator
self-report** — which removes the incentive to inflate difficulty.

| Item | Pay | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1** — clean read, single pass | **$22** | ~15 min active labor |
| **Tier 2** — cleaning + multi-pass | **$34** | the volume center of gravity, ~25–35 min |
| **Tier 3** — severe damage, heroic recovery | **$52** | 40+ min; **pays more than a clean disc earns → kills cherry-picking** |
| **Attempt fee** — QC-confirmed real multi-pass on a no-recovery disc | **$9** | keeps operators fighting hopeless discs even though Heirvo earns $0 there |
| **Monthly quality bonus** | **+8% of month's piece earnings** | requires QC pass-rate ≥ 97% AND on-time ≥ 95% |
| **Volume step-down** (orders of 20+ discs) | T1 $19 / T2 $29 / T3 $44 | protects Heirvo's margin where its per-disc price drops |

**Margin check (representative orders):**

- **1 disc (T2):** customer pays $108.99 → minus ~$3.46 processing, $34 operator, ~$8 handling
  → **Heirvo retains ~$63.53** (operator = 38% of recovery revenue).
- **5 discs (mix):** $407.99 → **Heirvo retains ~$221.86** (operator = 42%).
- **20 discs (mix, with step-down):** $1,199.99 → **Heirvo retains ~$604.89** (operator = 46%).

**Worked operator earnings** (mix: 35% T1 / 50% T2 / 15% T3, bonus earned, ~28 min active/disc):

| Discs/mo | Gross | ~Active hrs | ~Gross/hr |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 | ~$1,066 | 14 | ~$76 |
| 80 | ~$2,876 | 37 | ~$78 |
| 150 | ~$5,375 | 70 | ~$77 |

Net of self-employment tax + consumables ≈ **$60–64/hr take-home** — well above the ~$37/hr
1099 median, no driving, parallel machine time. First-month equipment cost is recovered inside
the first 30-disc month.

**Plain handbook version — "How you get paid":**
> You're paid per disc, by how hard it was: **easy $22, medium $34, hard $52.** Our software
> sets the difficulty from your recovery log — you never have to argue for it. **Couldn't
> recover a disc? You still get $9**, as long as your log shows a real full attempt. **Monthly
> bonus: +8% on everything** if you pass quality checks on 97%+ of discs and hit turnaround 95%+
> of the time. Careful and on-time = more money. (Orders of 20+ discs pay slightly lower
> per-disc rates because you run more per machine cycle.)

## 8. Chain of custody & quality control

**Mandatory, timestamped chain-of-custody** logged in the lab UI on every job:

1. **Scan-in on receipt** + **photo of disc condition on arrival** (protects operator *and*
   customer — pre-existing damage is documented).
2. Each processing step logged.
3. **Scan-out on return** with tracking #.

Use **bar-coded door-to-door tracking and a GPS/AirTag-style tracker in shipments** (ScanMyPhotos
precedent), including the return package. Insured + tracked shipping on **every** leg.

**QC mechanisms:**

- **Sampling that scales with trust:** 100% during probation → 50% → 20% → trusted-tier, with a
  **permanent random floor (5–10%) that never goes to zero.**
- **Photo/video verification** of unboxing and re-packing on every job.
- **Output spot-checks against ground truth** where Heirvo has reference data (byte/file compare).
- **Blind re-test discs:** periodically route a known-condition disc *disguised as a real
  customer job*. Their handling, output, and honesty are graded blind. **This is the single best
  detector of an operator who quietly started cutting corners once unsupervised.**
- **The irreplaceability rule (protocol step #1):** wherever feasible, originals are
  imaged/photographed *before* any recovery attempt, so a "lost" original is never truly the only
  copy. This is the ultimate backstop against the catastrophic failure mode.

## 9. Equipment

> **Buy your own gear from independent third parties (Amazon, Best Buy, the manufacturer) at
> market price. Heirvo never sells or supplies the kit and takes no vendor cut — this is what
> keeps the network out of franchise classification (§11).** Secondhand is fine to start.

**Why drive choice matters:** consumer playback *interpolates* (guesses) over unreadable spots;
recovery needs the opposite — a drive that reports errors honestly (C2 error pointers), exposes
raw read commands, and retries aggressively (no riplock). A single bad sector can need 10,000+
retries.

**The two-drive logic:** the Pioneer + LiteOn pair gives **two independent reading engines** —
a sector one drive can't read, the other often can, due to different lasers/optics/firmware.
Running a disc through both and merging recovered sectors is what pushes recovery from ~90% to
97%+.

- **Pioneer (primary):** killer feature is **PureRead** — set to **Master mode** (re-reads on
  error, interpolates only as last resort). *Never use Perfect mode on damaged discs — it aborts
  on the first hard error.* Models: BDR-2213 (~$119 new), BDR-S13U-X (PureRead 4+, ~$175),
  BDR-XD05 external. **Pioneer has exited the optical-drive business — these are trending
  secondhand-only; buy while available** (~$120–180 used).
- **LiteOn (secondary):** iHAS124 (~$45, still produced) — a cheap, different reading engine,
  also good for C2 quality scanning.
- **Plextor caveat:** the modern **"Plextor" PX-891SAF is a rebadged LiteOn — not a true
  Plextor; do not pay a premium for it.** The genuine accurate dumpers are *vintage* Plextors
  (PX-760A, PX-716A, PX-712, Premium) — IDE, need a PATA adapter, ~$30–150 used — the
  connoisseur's choice for accurate audio-CD / Photo-CD reads.

| Tier | Kit | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Starter (secondhand OK)** | Pioneer BDR-2213 or used XD05 (~$80–130) + LiteOn iHAS124 (~$45) + USB enclosure/adapter (~$15–25) | **~$140–200** |
| **Ideal** | Pioneer BDR-S13U-X (~$175) + LiteOn iHAS124 (~$45) + genuine vintage Plextor + PATA adapter (~$60–170) | **~$280–390** |

## 10. Cleaning & extraction SOP

**Cleaning solution:** ~70%+ **isopropyl alcohol** ~1:1 with **distilled water** (never tap —
mineral deposits).

**Technique — wipe direction is critical:** lightly dampen a **microfiber/cotton cloth** and
wipe **in straight radial lines, center outward — NEVER in circles.** Error correction tolerates
radial scratches but is defeated by a circular scratch following the data track. Dry, air-dry
fully before reading.

**Never use:** paper towels/tissues, abrasive cloths, ammonia/window cleaner, acetone, or
toothpaste/baking-soda "hacks" on irreplaceable discs.

- **Mold/fungus:** nitrile/vinyl gloves (not cotton); lift growth with an IPA cotton swab,
  dabbing not scrubbing; clean promptly (fungus etches the surface), then store separately in
  controlled humidity.
- **Disc rot** (bronzing, pinholes, transparent patches): *chemical* failure — cleaning won't
  fix it. Go straight to careful multi-drive multi-pass extraction.
- **Resurfacing** (deep read-side scratches only): **JFJ Easy Pro** (~$160–300). **Never
  resurface the label/top side** — the data layer is just under the label and is destroyed
  instantly. Last resort, after cleaning fails.

**Cleaning kit:** 99% IPA (~$8), distilled water (~$2), microfiber (~$8), nitrile gloves (~$10),
cotton swabs (~$3) — **basic ~$30**; with JFJ resurfacer **~$230**.

**Extraction SOP (handbook-ready, per disc):**

1. **Receive & log.** Assign job ID; gloves on; handle by edges + center hole only.
2. **Inspect & photograph** both sides under good light — scratches (radial vs circular),
   fingerprints, mold, label flaking, bronzing/pinholes, disc type (CD/DVD/Photo CD).
3. **Assess & route:** surface dirt → clean; mold → mold protocol; deep read-side scratches →
   flag for resurfacing; rot or label damage → skip cleaning, handle as fragile.
4. **Clean appropriately** (radial wipes; mold protocol if needed). Document before/after.
5. **Resurface only if needed** (JFJ, read side only); re-clean after.
6. **Mount in Pioneer**, PureRead in **Master mode**.
7. **First pass:** grab all easily-readable sectors fast and build a bad-sector map
   (`ddrescue -n -b 2048 /dev/srX out.iso map.log`, or IsoBuster on Windows). For Photo CDs,
   extract the full ISO; recover the `.PCD` images from inside it afterward.
8. **Targeted retry passes** on bad sectors only (`ddrescue -r3 ...`). Accept hours/days on
   heavily damaged discs.
9. **Swap to LiteOn**, run the *same* map so its different laser fills sectors the Pioneer
   couldn't. (Optional **third drive** — genuine Plextor — for irreplaceable Photo/audio CDs.)
10. **Persistent bad sectors:** re-clean, re-seat, slow read speed, re-run. Track recovered % per
    attempt.
11. **Verify integrity:** record MD5/SHA-256; mount the image and confirm files actually open.
    Note exactly which files (if any) are affected by remaining bad areas.
12. **Decide recoverable vs. not:** if multiple drives over multiple passes plateau, declare the
    affected files unrecoverable — and report the **specific % and which files** recovered.
    Partial recovery is still a win for family memories.
13. **Package results:** verified files + ISO + checksum + before/after photos + a short
    condition/recovery report (drives, passes, % recovered, lost files). Store the physical disc
    safely until return.

## 11. Legal guardrails (orientation, not legal advice)

**FTC Franchise Rule (16 CFR Part 436).** A relationship is a regulated "franchise" — triggering
Franchise Disclosure Documents + state registration — only if **all three** are present: (a) a
trademark/brand license, (b) significant control/assistance, **(c) a required payment of $500+ in
the first 6 months.** Heirvo clearly has (a) and (b). **The whole compliance strategy is defeating
element (c):**

- **Never sell or supply the equipment kit, and take no vendor referral cut.** The FTC's
  "required payment" reading is broad and includes "required equipment and supplies" bought *from
  the franchisor or its affiliate* — and the inventory exclusion covers *resale* inventory, **not
  equipment the operator uses to run the business.** If Heirvo supplied/marked-up the kit, that
  purchase could blow past $500 *by itself*. Operators buying generic gear on the open market
  stays outside the definition.
- **Deposits held by Heirvo count even if refundable.** Keep any deposit well under $500 and
  ideally neutrally held, not paid to Heirvo. Simplest: **$0 fee, no deposit.**
- **Don't charge for the trial — pay them (reduced rate).** Money flowing *to* the operator is
  never a "required payment," so the paid trial stays clean.
- **Language:** "licensed lab operator" / "independent regional lab" — never "franchisee,"
  "franchise fee," or "territory purchase." Territory is **granted/earned, never sold.**

**Worker classification (1099).** Territory + handbook + QC + routing is real control, raising
misclassification risk. The strict **ABC test** (CA/MA/NJ etc.) treats a worker as an employee
unless the hirer proves all three of: (A) freedom from control over *how* the work is done,
(B) work *outside* the hirer's usual business, (C) the worker runs an independent trade. Heirvo
fails **(B)** — disc recovery *is* its business — so in ABC states these operators look like
employees, and a 1099 label/agreement/LLC does **not** cure it. Mitigate by managing *outcomes*
(QC standards, deadlines) not minute-by-minute *process*: operators set their own hours, use
their own gear, may serve other clients, and the handbook is quality specs, not a supervision
manual.

**Get a lawyer before launch for:** trial-payment mechanics, any deposit, state-by-state
franchise *and* worker-classification exposure (CA, NJ, MA, NY + franchise-registration states),
and the operating-agreement language.

## 12. Territory model & phased rollout

**Territory unit:** **metro (CBSA/DMA)**, defined by a named ZIP cluster, so "regional lab near
you" maps to a customer's address. Because the service is mail-in, **territory is a
marketing/retention construct, not a physical service boundary** — any lab can process any disc,
which is the load-balancing superpower.

**Routing engine:**
- **Default:** route to the customer's home-metro lab (preserves the local promise).
- **Overflow:** if that lab is at capacity, **silently reroute to the nearest lab with
  capacity** — the customer never sees it, and the "one lab per city" promise is intact because
  Heirvo simply absorbs spillover elsewhere.
- **Capacity signals:** each operator reports live **open / busy / dark** status; the router
  avoids busy, never assigns to dark.
- **Lab goes dark mid-order:** a chain-of-custody state machine (intake → in-lab → in-QC →
  shipped) triggers a **recall/forward protocol** (originals ship to a designated overflow lab or
  regional hub); no new orders go to a non-responsive lab.

**Bench / no single point of failure:** keep a **waitlist of trial-passed but unassigned
operators** per region. Every active territory has a designated, pre-qualified backup. Cap each
metro at **one customer-facing operator** but keep **2–3 bench operators regionally** — that's how
you hold the "one lab per city" promise *and* eliminate single-point-of-failure at once. Run the
trial pipeline continuously so the bench never empties.

**Phased rollout:**
- **Phase 1 — Founding labs (8–12):** NY, LA, Chicago, Dallas–Fort Worth, Houston, Atlanta,
  Phoenix, Miami, Washington DC, Seattle. Favorable lease terms; they become proof-of-concept +
  overflow backbone.
- **Phase 2 — Top 25 (+~15):** Philadelphia, Boston, SF, Detroit, Minneapolis, Tampa, Denver,
  St. Louis, Charlotte, Orlando, etc.
- **Phase 3 — Top 50 + secondary markets** for full national "local lab" coverage.
